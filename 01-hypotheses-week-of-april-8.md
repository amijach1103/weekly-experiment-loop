# Hypotheses: week of April 8, 2026

**Work sample.** One week's hypothesis file from the pilot, written the day after the cohort's fourth session from the session transcript, that week's peer review comments in Google Docs, and the cohort's Slack channel. Founders are not named, and their quotes are paraphrased. "MR" is the weekly management report each founder writes in the partners' methodology.

---

## Last week's hypotheses: status

**Peer review scaffolding prompts: KEEP, iterate.** After the reminder started carrying four prompts (clarity, blind spots, distractions, resources), new reviewers appeared and comment volume rose. The open question is what the next iteration looks like, because the session itself reframed what comments are for (see the reflection-prompts observation below).

**Accountability nudges: KEEP, iterate.** Submissions and attendance rose the week the nudges started, and several participants said in the session that the reminders helped. In Slack, one founder said they liked the reminder's header but skipped the rest, and that the meeting summary was the most useful part. The nudges work as a trigger that something is due, not as content people read. Keep them short.

**AI-assisted peer review: EVOLVED.** The original idea was AI that explains unfamiliar domains to reviewers. In the session, a founder reframed what a comment is for: a reviewer's question is there to help you think, not a task you owe an answer to. So the AI should generate probing questions, not explanations.

**Async review over the live session for coaching: STRONGLY VALIDATED.** A founder said most of the coaching had already happened in the comments, and praised the agenda that came pre-filled with the problems from their reports. The live session is becoming a commitment ceremony and triage, not the main coaching venue.

**Automation hierarchy: VALIDATED and extended.** Summaries were valued above reminders, and the group asked for something higher still: a view of who is progressing and who is stuck. The order is now **dashboard > summaries > scaffolding > nudges**.

## Observations

### 1. Week-over-week trajectory is invisible
Several participants said independently that the reports show totals, not trajectory, so a reviewer has to do the math to see what happened this week. The group agreed to add week-by-week columns. The data already exists in each week's report; it just isn't presented side by side.

### 2. Comments are reflection prompts, not tasks
A founder said the questions in comments are there to help you think, so there's no need to spend time answering them. Another founder decided on the spot to stop replying to comments. The group defined peer review as reflection, not as accountability for answering.

### 3. Coaching is stage-specific
The group pushed the founder closest to market toward measuring product-market fit, and pushed the earlier-stage founders, me included, toward talking to customers first. Nobody prompted this; experienced members calibrated their advice by stage.

### 4. Avoiding customer conversations is the main blocker
Two participants said they hadn't talked to customers that week. The group responded with offers to find founders to talk to, a local meetup to try, and a push to write the hardest part down as a problem in next week's report. Peer review had flagged the same gap in writing before the session. The accountability worked, but only live.

### 5. The report as an experiment
A founder praised a report for reading like an experiment: a hypothesis for the week, the results measured, the next step. The group treated that as the standard every report should meet.

### 6. A dashboard is wanted
Founders proposed using the reports to work out who needs the most attention in the next session: who is moving and who is stuck. The group described an intelligence layer that reads the reports and suggests triage, which sits above summaries and nudges in value.

### 7. A first team member framing landed
A founder suggested that what the product offers is a first team member who happens to be AI. The group responded well. It's more concrete than "AI coaching tool" and fits a solo founder.

### 8. The weekly windows are too tight
A founder asked for an end-of-day report deadline instead of 6 PM, and the group said one day isn't enough for peer review. The Monday report, Tuesday review, Wednesday session rhythm works as a structure; the windows need to widen.

## New hypotheses

### H7: Week-over-week trajectory view
**If we** add weekly comparison columns to the Key Indicators section of the report, **then** peer review quality will rise and session triage will become data-driven, **because** several founders independently named missing trajectory as the main barrier to useful feedback.

*Assumptions:*
1. The barrier is partly "can't see trajectory", not only "don't know what to say". Scaffolding addressed the second; this addresses the first.
2. Founders will keep the columns up each week.
3. The numbers move enough to show a trajectory. For very early founders, they may not.

*Build scope:* add weekly columns to the template; have the agent pre-fill last week's numbers from the previous report; draft a cross-cohort trajectory summary for the facilitator.

### H8: Stage-aware coaching
**If we** design the AI coaching layer to detect a founder's stage (idea, build, market) and surface prompts for that stage, **then** coaching will feel more relevant, **because** the group already coaches differently by stage.

*Assumptions:*
1. Stage can be detected from what a founder writes in the report.
2. Stage-specific prompts beat generic ones. It could also be that the human judgment in live coaching is the value, and packaged prompts feel formulaic.
3. The stages in this cohort generalize to other cohorts.

*Build scope:* exploratory. Not ready for a spec this week; it needs the UX researcher's view on whether this is a product feature or a facilitator tool.

### H9: The report as an experiment template
**If we** make the experiment framing explicit in the report template (this week's hypothesis, actions, results, next experiment), **then** reports will converge faster on the format the group praised, **because** the group named it as the standard.

*Assumptions:*
1. Template cues can teach an experiment mindset. It may instead come only from coaching.
2. Every stage benefits. Founders still defining their project may struggle to frame experiments.
3. The existing sections can take experiment language without confusing people who learned the original format.

*Build scope:* light. Keep the partners' sections and reword the headers: "What my experiments showed", "This week's experiments", "What's blocking my experiments (and what help I need)", "The one experiment that matters most".

### H10: Customer conversation accountability
**If we** build a mechanism that tracks and nudges customer conversations, not just report submissions, **then** the cohort will produce market signal faster, **because** customer conversations are the highest-leverage activity for early founders, and the group's intervention this week worked live but not async.

*Assumptions:*
1. The barrier is mostly avoidance, not access. The session suggests both: one founder had leads and hadn't started; another offered to find people.
2. A template change or a bot can replicate what happened live. The live version worked because of peers pushing in real time, which a bot message may not carry.
3. This applies to early founders generally, not just this cohort.

*Build scope, three options for the review:*
- **A, template:** a "customer conversations this week" field in the report (did you talk to anyone, how many, what you learned).
- **B, bot:** a Wednesday morning prompt asking who talked to a customer this week.
- **C, peer matching:** connect founders who need conversations with members who can make introductions.

## Questions for the UX researcher

1. Trajectory (H7): template change, agent feature or dashboard? What's the right first step?
2. Comments as reflection: how does this change AI-assisted review? Should the AI ask "Have you considered…" instead of explaining?
3. Customer conversations (H10): can the live accountability be replicated async, or is this what the live session is uniquely good at?
4. Stage-aware coaching (H8): design it into the product, or leave it to human judgment?
5. Experiment template (H9): helpful, or over-structured for founders still exploring?
6. The automation hierarchy: does community research support dashboards > summaries > scaffolding > nudges?
