# A weekly experiment loop for an AI product

Working documents from an early-stage AI product for entrepreneurs that I led at a research and development lab in 2025 and 2026. We were testing whether AI could help a peer cohort of founders get what an in-person entrepreneurship program gives them: a weekly report, peer review, and a live accountability session. The methodology came from our program partners. I studied it; my job was to test how AI could help it reach more people. People's names, program names and details of the founders' ventures are removed.

## The loop

The pilot ran on a weekly cycle:

- **Wednesday:** observe the cohort's live session.
- **Thursday:** write hypotheses from what we saw, as "If we… then… because…", with the assumptions written out.
- **Friday:** write a design spec for each feature, with success and kill criteria set before the test.
- **Monday and Tuesday:** build it.
- **The next Wednesday:** test it in the session against those criteria.

I built the features with Claude Code, through an instance scoped to the cohort's Slack workflows, and reviewed the message copy before it deployed. I wrote each spec for a UX researcher and a colleague who studies community dynamics to review; their input is in the specs, credited by role. I also took part in the cohort myself, to learn the methodology firsthand.

## The documents

1. [Hypotheses, one week](01-hypotheses-week-of-april-8.md): what we observed in a session and in the week's peer review, the status of the previous week's hypotheses, and four new ones with their assumptions and build scope.
2. [Design specs, week 1](02-design-specs-week-1.md): four Slack automations for the cohort, each with what it does, why we thought it would work, the exact message copy, success and kill criteria, and the assumptions it tested.
3. [Design specs and decisions, a later week](03-design-specs-and-decisions.md): a two-tier dashboard, a session structure and a template prompt, followed by the six decisions locked after review.

## How it ended

I recommended against another cycle, and leadership decided to shelve the project.
